Facebook Page Roles Explained: Admin, Editor, Moderator and More — Who Should Get What Access

By Md Alamin Islam · 22/10/2026
In this article
  1. Why roles matter
  2. The common roles
  3. How to add someone
  4. How to remove or change a role
  5. Good habits
  6. Warning signs

If you run a Facebook Page with other people, you must decide who gets which role. Giving too much access is one of the most common reasons Pages are hijacked or damaged. This guide explains the roles in plain language and how to add people safely. Menu names and screens may change over time, and the newer Pages experience may use "full control" and "partial access" instead of old role names.

Why roles matter

A Page can hold your audience, your ads and your income. One careless admin can delete posts, remove other people or change settings. Giving each person only the access they need keeps the Page safe.

The common roles

  • Admin: the highest level. Can manage roles, settings, posts, messages and ads. Can also remove others.
  • Editor: can create posts, reply to comments, send messages and see Insights. Cannot usually manage roles.
  • Moderator: can reply to comments and messages and remove unwanted comments. Limited control over content.
  • Advertiser: can create and manage ads and see Insights.
  • Analyst: can only see Insights.

In newer Pages, you may see "Facebook access" with full control or partial control, and separate task settings. The idea is the same: give the least access that does the job.

How to add someone

1. Switch into your Page profile. 2. Open Settings and look for Page access or Page roles. 3. Choose to add a new person and search for their name or Facebook profile. 4. Pick the role or the tasks they should have. 5. Confirm with your password if asked. The person must accept the invite.

How to remove or change a role

1. Go to the same Page access settings. 2. Find the person's name. 3. Change their role or remove their access. 4. If you suspect a problem, remove the person first and then change your own password.

Good habits

  • Keep at least two trusted full-control people, so you are not locked out if one account has a problem.
  • Give new people the lowest role first, then increase it when you trust them.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for every admin account.
  • Review the list every few months and remove people who no longer work with you.
  • Never give admin access to someone who contacts you out of the blue, even if they promise more followers or income.

Warning signs

  • You get a message saying you were added or removed as admin that you did not expect.
  • Someone asks you to add them to the Page "for verification".
  • Settings changed without your knowledge.

If any of these happen, change your password, review the active sessions, and remove unknown people right away. If you lose access to your own Page, contact us through the form and we will explain the official steps. We cannot promise results, and we are not representatives of Meta.
