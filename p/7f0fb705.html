Forgot Your WhatsApp Two-Step Verification PIN? How to Reset It Safely

Two-step verification adds a 6-digit PIN to your WhatsApp account. It protects you if someone gets your SMS code. But many people forget the PIN, and then they cannot register WhatsApp on a new phone. Here is what the PIN does, how to reset it, and how to avoid scams. Menu names and screens may change over time.

What the PIN is for

When you register your number on a new phone, WhatsApp sends an SMS code. If two-step verification is on, WhatsApp also asks for your 6-digit PIN. This stops a stranger from taking over your number even if they see your SMS code. WhatsApp may also ask for the PIN from time to time to help you remember it.

If you still have WhatsApp open on your old phone

1. Open WhatsApp and go to Settings. 2. Tap Account, then Two-step verification. 3. Tap Change PIN and set a new 6-digit PIN. 4. Add or confirm your recovery email. This email is very important.

If you forgot the PIN on a new phone

1. On the PIN screen, tap Forgot PIN. 2. WhatsApp sends a reset link to the email you added earlier. Check inbox and spam folder. 3. Open the link and confirm. Then try registering again. 4. If you never added an email, WhatsApp does not let you reset right away. You must wait 7 days since your last use of the number. After that, you can register again, but old pending messages may be lost.

Common problems

  • Email not arriving: check spam, check that you are looking at the correct email, and wait a few minutes.
  • No access to the email: you may have to wait for the 7-day period.
  • Link not working: request a new one and open it on the same phone.
  • Message "too many attempts": wait a while before you try again.

Stay safe from scams

  • Never share your SMS code or PIN with anyone, even a friend or someone claiming to be from WhatsApp. WhatsApp will not ask for your code.
  • A message that says "I sent you a code by mistake, please send it back" is a common trick to steal accounts.
  • Do not click reset links that arrive from unknown senders. Use only the email you added yourself.
  • Turn on two-step verification and add a recovery email you can really access.

Tips for the future

  • Write the PIN in a safe place, such as a password manager.
  • Use a PIN that is not your birth year or phone number.
  • Keep your email account secure with a strong password and its own two-step verification.
