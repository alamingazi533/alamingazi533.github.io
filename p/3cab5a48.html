YouTube Shorts Monetization Explained — Requirements, How Shorts Revenue Works and What to Do First

Many creators in Bangladesh and elsewhere start with YouTube Shorts because short videos are easy to make. But earning money from Shorts requires meeting YouTube's rules, and the income works differently from regular videos. This guide explains the general requirements of the YouTube Partner Program (YPP), how Shorts revenue is shared, and a practical plan to get started. YouTube changes its rules and country availability, so confirm everything in the Earn section of YouTube Studio.

What the YouTube Partner Program is

YPP is the program that lets eligible channels earn money from ads and other features. Being in YPP is not automatic. You must apply once you meet the requirements, and YouTube reviews your channel.

Main requirements YouTube has published

  • At least 1,000 subscribers.
  • Plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days.
  • Your channel must follow YouTube's policies, including monetization and copyright rules, and have no active Community Guidelines strike.
  • Two-step verification must be on for the Google account.
  • You must live in a country or region where YPP is available. Bangladesh has been listed as supported, but check your own channel in YouTube Studio.

YouTube may also offer a lower early tier in some regions that unlocks features like fan support before full ad revenue. That tier may not be available everywhere or may change, so look at what Studio shows for your channel.

How Shorts earn money

Shorts ad revenue is not paid per video like long-form ads in the same way. In general, ads shown in the Shorts feed create a pool of revenue. After certain costs such as music licensing, the remainder is shared with creators based on their share of Shorts views. This is why the income per thousand views is often lower than for long videos. Anyone who promises an exact amount per view is guessing.

A practical plan

Step 1: Choose a clear topic, such as tech tips, cooking, education or local services. Step 2: Make original videos. Do not repost other people's clips. Step 3: Hook viewers in the first one or two seconds. Step 4: Keep a regular schedule, for example four to five Shorts a week. Step 5: Use music from YouTube's own library or music you have a license for. Step 6: Add clear titles and a few relevant keywords or tags in the description. Step 7: Study YouTube Studio analytics to see which videos keep viewers. Step 8: Mix in a few longer videos if they fit your topic. They can help build watch hours and loyal subscribers. Step 9: When you qualify, apply in the Earn tab and connect AdSense.

Money and tax details for Bangladeshi creators

You will need an AdSense account with correct name and address, and address verification through a PIN sent by post. Payment depends on the bank and on tax information Google may ask for. Check bank charges and currency conversion before you plan around earnings.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not buy views or subscribers. It breaks policy and can lead to rejection or termination. Do not reuse other creators' videos or copyrighted songs. Do not trust courses claiming guaranteed monetization in days. Do not ignore a copyright claim or strike.

Be realistic

Reaching 10 million Shorts views or 4,000 watch hours is hard for most new channels. Treat monetization as a long-term goal. Also think about other income, such as sponsorships, selling your own services or products, or building a website.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need both Shorts views and watch hours? You need subscribers plus either the watch-hour condition or the Shorts-view condition.

Are views from a few countries worth more? Revenue can differ by audience and ad demand, but there is no fixed rate.
