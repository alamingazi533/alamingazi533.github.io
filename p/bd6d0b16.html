YouTube "Made for Kids" Setting Explained — What It Changes, How to Set It Correctly and How to Fix a Wrong Label

If you upload videos to YouTube, you must tell YouTube whether each video is "Made for Kids". Many creators ignore this setting or choose it carelessly, and later they wonder why comments disappeared or why their video earns less. This guide explains what the setting means, what changes when it is turned on, and how to correct a wrong choice. YouTube updates its policies, so confirm the details in YouTube Studio and Help Center.

What "Made for Kids" means

This is an audience setting. It is not about whether a video is simply clean or family friendly. It is a legal and policy label that YouTube uses to follow children's privacy laws, such as COPPA in the United States. A video is made for kids if it is directed at children, for example nursery rhymes, toy videos or cartoons aimed at young viewers. It can also be marked if children are among the likely audience.

What changes when a video is Made for Kids

  • Comments are turned off.
  • Personalised ads are not shown, which can reduce earnings.
  • Some features, such as notifications, saving to a playlist in certain ways, and cards or end screens, may be limited.
  • The video may appear in kid-focused areas of YouTube.

These limits protect children's privacy, but they also affect how the video performs for a creator.

Not Made for Kids

If your video is not directed at children, choose "No, it's not made for kids." You can still make an age-restricted video if the content is suitable only for adults. Be honest and accurate, because the label is a legal matter.

Where to set it

  • Open YouTube Studio.
  • Go to Content, then open a video.
  • Scroll to the Audience section.
  • Choose Yes or No for Made for Kids.

For the whole channel, you can set a default under Settings, Channel, Advanced settings. The default applies to new uploads, but you should still review each video.

YouTube can change the setting

YouTube may use its systems to review and change a video's audience setting if it thinks you have chosen incorrectly. If this happens, you may see a notice in YouTube Studio. If you disagree, there is usually an option to ask for a review.

How to fix a wrong label

  • Open the video in YouTube Studio and check the Audience section.
  • If you chose Made for Kids by mistake and your video is not for children, change it to No and save. The setting can update, and comments may return after processing.
  • If YouTube set it for you, use the option shown on the video page to appeal.
  • Wait a few days. Changes may take time to reflect.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Do not mark a video "not for kids" just to keep comments or ads if it is truly aimed at children. This can bring policy action.
  • Do not use cartoons or toys in a thumbnail if your content is for adults. It may confuse the review.
  • Do not guess. When unsure, read YouTube's current guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Question: Does Made for Kids remove all monetisation? Answer: It limits personalised ads, which can lower revenue, but it does not necessarily remove monetisation completely.

Question: Can I change the setting later? Answer: Yes. You can edit the audience setting in YouTube Studio at any time, though processing may take a little while.

Question: Why are my comments gone? Answer: Comments are turned off automatically on videos marked Made for Kids. Check the Audience setting on that video.
