How to Spot Facebook Marketplace Scams — Fake Payments, Overpayment Tricks and Safe Ways to Buy and Sell

Facebook Marketplace is a convenient place to buy and sell, but it is also a favourite place for scammers. The tricks are repeated again and again, so once you learn the pattern, most scams become easy to spot. This guide covers the most common Marketplace scams, the warning signs, and the safe habits that protect your money and your account. Facebook can change its rules, so use the in-app tools when you report a listing.

Why scammers use Marketplace

Marketplace connects strangers quickly, and many people want a deal. A scammer only needs a convincing listing or a convincing message. They often work from fake or hacked accounts, so a profile that looks real is not proof of safety.

Common scams when you are the buyer

  • Too good to be true prices. A phone or motorbike at half its normal price, with a story about urgent sale or travelling abroad.
  • Advance payment. The seller asks you to send money first, promising delivery later. After you pay, they vanish.
  • Fake delivery or courier. A "courier" asks you to pay a fee or tax before the item arrives.
  • Moving off the platform. The seller insists on talking on WhatsApp or another app, away from Facebook's protection.
  • Stolen photos. The pictures are copied from other listings. You can test this by searching the photos online.

Common scams when you are the seller

  • Fake payment screenshot. The buyer sends a screenshot of a bKash, Nagad or bank transfer, but no money has actually arrived. Always check your own account balance or message, never the screenshot.
  • Overpayment. The buyer "accidentally" sends more than the price and asks you to return the difference. Later the original payment is reversed or turns out to be fake.
  • Code requests. The buyer says they need to verify you are real and asks you to share a code that arrives by SMS. This code can be used to take over your accounts. Never share it.
  • Suspicious links. A buyer sends a link to "confirm payment" or "receive money". The page steals your login or card details.

Safe habits for buyers

  • Meet in a public place and inspect the item before paying.
  • Pay only after you receive the item, or use a trusted cash-on-delivery arrangement.
  • Check the seller's profile age, other listings and reviews.
  • Do not click payment links sent in messages.
  • Compare the price with similar items. A big gap is a warning.

Safe habits for sellers

  • Accept payment only when you can see the money in your own account.
  • Never send the item before the payment has cleared.
  • Never share OTPs, passwords or card numbers.
  • Do not return "extra" money until you are completely sure the original payment is real.

What to do if you are scammed

  • Stop all contact and keep screenshots of the chat, listing and payment details.
  • Contact your bank or mobile payment service immediately and explain what happened.
  • Report the listing and the profile to Facebook using the Report option.
  • Block the scammer.
  • If you shared a code or password, change your passwords and turn on two-factor authentication.
  • Report serious fraud to the local authorities.

Frequently asked questions

Question: Is Marketplace safe to use at all? Answer: Yes, if you stay careful. Most problems come from advance payment, fake screenshots and requests for codes.

Question: Can Facebook refund my money? Answer: Facebook generally does not handle payments for local cash deals, so a refund is unlikely. Prevention is far more reliable than recovery.

Question: What if the buyer asks me to ship to another city? Answer: Be careful. Accept only payment you can verify, and never ship before it clears.
